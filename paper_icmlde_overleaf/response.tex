Dear Scientific Committee and Reviewers,

We thank the reviewers for their careful reading and constructive comments. We have revised the manuscript and respond to every comment below, quoting each comment in italics and giving what we changed and where. The main changes are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Larger evaluation} (\sect{sec:setup}, \sect{sec:results}): three Amazon Reviews 2023 datasets with up to 50,000 users, reporting Recall, NDCG, MAP, and MRR at $K\in\{10,20\}$ (plus HitRate and Precision) with significance tests (\tabl{tab:datasets}), and 17 prototype scenarios instead of 5 (\tabl{tab:results}).

  \item \textbf{Format and language}: Procedia Computer Science template, redrawn figures, shortened captions, and proofreading of the entire manuscript.
\end{itemize}

\point{R3}{Specify LightGCN's training settings, including layers, embedding size, negative sampling, and stopping criteria.}
\response The settings are now stated in the text and in Table~\ref{tab:settings}: 64-dimensional embeddings, 3 propagation layers (averaged), one uniformly sampled negative per positive pair (resampled if observed), Adam with learning rate $10^{-3}$ and $L_2$ weight $10^{-4}$, batches of 4,096 pairs, and a fixed 60 epochs without early stopping, so that validation and test data do not influence training.
\where{Section 3.6, Eqs.~(5)--(7) (p.~6).}{\sect{sec:lightgcn}, \eqn{eq:lightgcn}, \eqn{eq:bpr}; \tabl{tab:settings}.}

\point{R3}{Clarify how positive interactions are selected and how different event types are weighted.}
\response Event weights are now listed: view 0.4, click 0.6, like and bookmark 1.0, complete 1.2, skip $-1.0$, and an explicit rating $r$ maps to $(r-3)/2$. An interaction is positive, and becomes a LightGCN edge, when its weight is positive; repeated events are merged into one edge, and skips and low ratings are excluded. For the Amazon benchmark, ratings of four or five stars are positive.
\where{Section 3.5 (p.~6); Section 3.6 (p.~6).}{\sect{sec:ema}; \sect{sec:lightgcn}; \tabl{tab:settings}.}

\point{R3}{Explain how the EMA profile is initialized and how skips or other negative events update it.}
\response The profile is empty until a user's first non-neutral event, which sets it to the item embedding (or its negation for a negative event); users without events receive no EMA score. Each event with weight $w$ moves the profile by the step $\alpha\min(|w|,1)$ with $\alpha=0.25$, toward the item embedding for positive events and toward its negation for skips, and the profile is then renormalized to unit length (\eqn{eq:ema_update}).
\where{Section 3.5, Eqs.~(3)--(4) (p.~6).}{\sect{sec:ema}, \eqn{eq:ema_update}.}

\point{R3}{Provide a precise procedure for constructing KG entities, relations, and user-item paths.}
\response The knowledge-graph subsection now gives a three-step procedure: (i) entities (items typed by content type; creator, genre, and term entities from the catalog text; user and profile nodes), (ii) relations (directed\_by, written\_by, performed\_by, belongs\_to\_genre, liked, bookmarked, completed, and the profile expansions with examples), and (iii) length-two user--entity--item paths, which are counted by set intersection in the KG and profile scores. Figure~\ref{fig:knowledge_graph} was redrawn to match.
\where{Section 3.7, Eqs.~(9)--(11), Fig.~2 (p.~7).}{\sect{sec:kg}; \eqn{eq:paths}, \eqn{eq:kg_score}, \eqn{eq:profile_score}; \figr{fig:knowledge_graph}.}

The rate is 8/17 overall and 8/11 for the scenarios without a domain filter (6/17 and 6/11 if movies and TV shows count as one medium); the six domain-scoped scenarios stay within their declared domain by design.
